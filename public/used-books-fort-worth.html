Used books for $5, $10 and $20 — everybody finds something.

Novels and memoirs. Biographies and cookbooks. Picture books and chapter books for kids. All used, all inspected, all in one of three price tiers — because a bookstore should be somewhere a family can shop without doing math.

The pricing, explained by its inventor

Three tiers, zero tantrums.

The tier system came from Dana's own kids. Two children, one book each — but only if "one book" never means "the most expensive one on the shelf."

$5

Paperbacks, chapter books and most of what kids reach for. Shoppers regularly spot nice chapter books here and ask if they're really $5. Yes.

$10

Middle-shelf finds — memoirs, biographies, cookbooks, the paperback hardbacks of books people are talking about.

$20

The special ones. The priciest book in the shop still costs less than most new hardbacks cost anywhere.

"If I know those books are $5, I can tell my children they can pick out two books, any two books... everybody's happy. Nobody got anything more expensive than the other."

Dana St. Germain, as told to the Fort Worth Report · November 2026

Curated, not sorted

"No — but I have that book adjacent."

Dana stocks what she calls "books adjacent" to your request: the off-the-beaten-path title, the forgotten one, the lost-in-the-shuffle one. She also carries whatever she's curious about — the shop's very first order included a Tiger King memoir, on purpose, because "I need to know what everybody else is reading."

  • Ask for anything — the shop's philosophy is to find you something close, not shrug at you
  • Stock rotates constantly; used means every copy is a different copy
  • Local authors get a shelf, and the shop says yes to authors who want to give something back to the community
  • Little gifts round it out: stickers, shirts, and notebooks with colored pencils for $1

Signature program

This Book Made Me Think of You.

The monthly gifting program the shop is known for — named after the line every good gift really is.

  1. Tell Dana about the person: what they're into, what they're going through, what they loved reading last.
  2. She picks the right book from the shop's own shelves.
  3. The book comes wrapped, with a handwritten note — yours or hers — tucked inside.
  4. You pick it up at the counter each month. Give it away. Repeat until they're hooked.

It started with a non-reader calling Dana for hours about his wife — she was postpartum and struggling — and asking the shop to find her books. He hand-wrote each note: "Here's why this book made me think of you." It's been the shop's favorite program ever since.

For the little readers

Kids are staff, regulars, and welcome.

This is a bookstore where the owners' own 11- and 8-year-olds run the register — and where other people's kids do the same after school.

  • Picture books and chapter books in every bin, priced so a kid can actually pick their own
  • Friday Friends — a former Montessori teacher hangs out Thursday mornings and ends up holding babies, to everyone's delight
  • After-school third space: friends are always welcome, always safe, and a craft is usually on the table
